Ongoing · day 2 ↗

Voting began on Friday in Russia's first parliamentary (State Duma) election since the full-scale invasion of Ukraine, with polls opening in the Far East and a three-day voting period underway across the country. Bank of Japan raises rates to 31-year high of 1.25%, but yen falls anyway

The Bank of Japan raised its benchmark interest rate by 0.25 percentage points to 1.25%, the highest level in 31 years and its first hike since June, as it moves away from decades of ultra-low rates to counter inflation running above its 2% target. Two board members opposed the decision, and the yen weakened after the announcement despite hawkish comments from Governor Kazuo Ueda, as investors had expected a faster pace of tightening. The move follows the U.S. Federal Reserve's rate rise earlier this week and comes with the European Central Bank's key rate already at 2.5%.

Why it matters

A yen that weakens even as the BoJ hikes suggests markets see Japan still lagging the Fed and ECB on rates; analysts warn that gap could keep pushing the currency down and import costs up, undermining the same inflation fight the rate rise is meant to win.

US approves $24.3bn F-35 sale to Saudi Arabia over Israeli, China concerns

The US State Department approved a $24.3 billion sale of 48 F-35 stealth jets to Saudi Arabia on Thursday, clearing Riyadh's long-standing effort to acquire the world's most advanced fighter jet and making it the first Arab country approved to operate the plane. The deal, Saudi Arabia's latest major arms purchase since its war with Iran began, still needs to clear a 30-day congressional review; Democratic Representative Raja Krishnamoorthi opposes it, citing US intelligence warnings that China could access F-35 technology through Riyadh's military ties with Beijing. Israel, the only Middle East country currently flying F-35s, has also raised concerns, though Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's office says understandings with Washington will preserve its military edge.

Why it matters

The sale tests two pillars of long-standing US Middle East policy at once: a legal guarantee that Israel keeps a qualitative military edge over its neighbors, and an effort to keep F-35 technology out of Chinese hands, which US intelligence officials warn Saudi Arabia's expanding military ties with Beijing could put at risk.
