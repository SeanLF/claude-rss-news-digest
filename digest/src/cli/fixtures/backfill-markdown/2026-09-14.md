### Sweden's centre-left extends lead to three seats as far right loses ground for first time since 2010

Ongoing · day 5

Sweden's centre-left opposition bloc, led by Social Democrat Magdalena Andersson, is projected to narrowly win the election, with preliminary results showing a lead of one to three seats over Prime Minister Ulf Kristersson's right-wing bloc, though the race remains too close to call with final votes still being counted. The far-right Sweden Democrats lost votes compared to their previous showing — the first time the party has lost support at a Swedish election since entering parliament — dashing hopes of a dominant performance and appearing to stumble on election night. Both Prime Minister Kristersson and opposition leader Andersson declared themselves ready to govern as counting continued, signalling that protracted coalition negotiations lie ahead regardless of the final outcome.

**Why it matters**

Kristersson had pledged to give the hard-right Sweden Democrats, founded by Nazi sympathisers in the 1980s, cabinet posts for the first time if his coalition held power; that outcome now hinges on Wednesday's recount of the roughly 28,000 votes separating the two blocs.
